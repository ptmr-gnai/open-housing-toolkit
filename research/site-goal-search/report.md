# Search findings and next practitioner review

The selection process begins with nonhousing that could be investigated for
housing: zero recorded homes is eligible, including retail, parking and auto uses.
Existing residential FAR capacity, a minimum lot size and auto use are not broad
admission requirements. Desired programs imply required area and FAR; they do not
establish a permitted envelope. Original use and zoning descriptors remain visible.

## Evidence and conditional results

Across 3,164 lots, the broad inventory retains 969 recorded nonhousing lots:
457 Jerome and 512 Hunts Point. Four housing sources return 117 lots with positive
historical/program signals, 506 without a returned positive signal, and 346 with
coverage unknown. These categories do not prove current occupancy or vacancy.
The monthly zoning source is reconciled against PLUTO without overwriting it;
two Jerome nonhousing lots lack a matching monthly record and require review.
See [source review](../nonhousing-discovery/report.md).

The declared baseline tests 40, 60 and 100 proposed homes at two illustrative land
rates. Other declared filters include station-point distance, zoning/review path,
industrial-policy context, housing-history coverage, active linked planning
projects, required FAR, rent, capital gap per home and annual cash flow. All failed
and unknown trials remain available; Pareto membership is conditional on three
explicit metrics, with no claim of universally best sites.

These are analyst scenario matches, not viable-site findings. In particular,
$2,000 year-one rent grows to $2,913.62 in year 20 under the baseline assumption.
Station-point distance is not walking distance. Land and construction rates are
illustrative, with no automatic market valuation or committed financing.
Changing costs by 10% eliminating Jerome matches is a material modeling result.

## A connected non-auto case

1530 University Avenue (2028750045), recorded K1 retail, illustrates a 60-home
program flowing from search to an independent rental model and existing area
analysis. It assumes 54,901.97 gross sf, required FAR proxy 5.630971, development
cost $39,580,887.68, modeled primary loan $8,267,062.62 and unallocated capital
$471,897.08 per proposed home. The baseline reviewed record has no returned
positive housing signal; the deeper capture retains fresh individual source
responses and its own dates rather than retroactively changing that baseline.

The mapped parcel computes about 10,700 sf versus recorded lot area 9,750 sf.
Both values remain visible. A separately authored schematic uses 13 floors and
an arbitrary 8-foot geometric edge margin; neither is a lawful envelope finding.
The uniform cost/sf model does not capture tower complexity. The area module's
60.000010 home-equivalents are arithmetic, not delivered whole homes.

## What to resolve with Jeremy

Review whether the actual nonhousing examples and selection knobs match his
intent. Occupancy/business displacement, title and willingness to sell, parcel
assembly, environmental constraints, lawful bulk, architecture, localized costs
and funding eligibility still require better evidence or expert review. Unknown
coverage stays a review queue. Do not interpret a no-positive historical query as
proof that no one lives there or that a business can be displaced.

[BetaNYC registry probes](../public-data-registry/README.md) provide discovery
of additional publisher datasets, including successful Buffalo, Rochester and
Syracuse API probes. Those probes are resource discovery, not an implemented
cross-city parcel-selection model. The NYC adapter and typed modeling contracts
are the working v1.
