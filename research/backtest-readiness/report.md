# Assumptions review and a real-project backtest

7 October 2026. A fresh-context AI reviewer found material gaps in the one-site conclusion, including a housing signal already present in the cached source. The engineering checks pass, but the assumptions need revision before this becomes a preferred-action, capacity or financial benchmark. A stronger real-project comparison is now grounded in public records for 425 Grand Concourse. A new backtest has not been executed.

## What the review changes

The full review is preserved separately from the frozen experiment. It is an AI critique, not housing-professional acceptance or the unfamiliar-human test.

| Finding | Consequence |

| Occupancy-first is pinned author policy | It is a reasonable task, but no comparison of cost, timing and stop conditions establishes it as the best next task. Compare title/control, zoning and environmental checks for the intended practitioner. |

| The displayed additional gap omits assumed baseline non-debt capital | Approximately $46.0 million baseline plus $45.7 million additional means $91.6 million of modeled non-debt capital before excluded costs. A closed extra gap does not mean a funded project. |

The reviewer reran all 20 tests and byte replay successfully. It also changed assumed average net home size from 850 to 700 sf: illustrated homes rose from 271 to 329 and modeled debt-support rent fell from about $3,869 to $3,187. This is sensitivity to an assumption, not evidence that smaller homes fit or rents remain unchanged.

These are useful failures. Exact citations did not stop relevant evidence being omitted, and connected dependencies did not establish a superior investigation order. The original outputs remain frozen so the improvement can later be assessed rather than rewriting the first result.

## What we have already backtested

The earlier 97 West 169th Street exercise used a real dated HPD solicitation and addenda, froze the assessment before reference comparison, and identified a missed applicant-eligibility requirement. It tested document assessment. It did not reconstruct actual design, closed financing, construction delivery or practitioner time savings. The later 100-home designation summary was a plan, not a submitted proposal or underwriting record.

A newly checked [WSFSSH building page](https://wsfssh.org/building/fischer-apartments/) reports 105 residences including a superintendent unit and an opening year of 2025. This enriches the later reference; it cannot be fed back into an April 2019 input packet or treated as proof that a particular early unit estimate was correct.

## Why 425 Grand Concourse is a stronger next reference

[HPD’s April 28, 2016 selection announcement](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/news/015-16/mayor-de-blasio-hpd-commissioner-been-hdc-president-rodney-development-team-will) describes a 241-home plan. [CPC’s November 2017 zoning-map report](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/cpc/180031.pdf) and [related zoning-text report](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/cpc/180033.pdf) document the proposed/approved commission actions, including the C4-4 to C6-3 change. These reports are later than the 2015 solicitation and do not by themselves establish every final legal effective date.

[HDC’s November 2, 2022 opening announcement](https://www.nychdc.com/newsroom/cutting-edge-425-grand-concourse-building-brings-277-affordable-apartments-and-holistic) reports 277 apartments in a $178 million mixed-use development and identifies tax-credit equity, tax-exempt bonds and public subsidies. This is a more realistic affordable-housing capital structure than the generic debt illustration. Detailed loan terms and a verified complete sources-and-uses remain unavailable in this packet.

[NYSERDA’s case description](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/About/Publications/Featured-Case-Studies/Buildings-of-Excellence-425-Grand-Concourse) reports 310,758 sf, a figure labeled Project Cost of $121,942,998 and $392.41 per gross sf. The cost scope needs reconciliation with HDC’s $178 million development figure before either is used as a financial benchmark. Its energy-model conditioned area is not apartment net area. It also describes rock and subway-adjacent construction constraints. These case particulars cannot supply a universal 2026 construction rate for another parcel.

The project is now openly known to us. Use it first as a calibration and reconstruction case. Do not call it an untouched holdout. Our Edward L Grant Highway illustration of 271 homes and this different building’s 277 apartments are not comparable unit-count predictions.

## The next experiment

The hypothesis is that the playbook can reconstruct the material entitlement, physical, program and funding conditions for a real pursuit, and identify a defensible next diligence action with appropriately bounded uncertainty.

1. Fix one historical decision date and recover only the documents and rules available then. Keep the 2016 selection, 2017 approval reports, 2022 opening and later cost descriptions in a separate reference packet. If historical inputs cannot be certified, label those dimensions untestable.
2. Freeze assumptions, area/cost definitions, numerical ranges, stop conditions and scoring before the next analyst output. Require a sources-and-uses structure that includes all non-debt funding and explicit missing commitments. Report counts by program, date and unit definition.
3. Compare next actions against an independent practitioner’s sequencing judgment when available. Evaluate material missed constraints, unsupported clearances, justified holds and traceability separately. A recommendation to investigate or stop can be correct; blanket abstention is not automatically a success.
4. Freeze the generated assessment, then reveal the real design/approval/financing trajectory. Explain differences as model error, different definitions, later program or rule changes, unavailable information, or unresolved causes. Do not tune FAR share or average home size until the count equals 277.
5. Preserve corrections in a new version and test them on another untouched case. Include a documented unsuccessful, delayed or changed case when found; absence from a designation archive does not prove failure.

The next immediate slice is a versioned assumption revision: make the omitted historical housing signal visible, show total required capital rather than only the additional gap, and compare investigation priorities. Then use the real-project reference to challenge those choices. Full geometric feasibility, financing prediction and practitioner usefulness remain open.
