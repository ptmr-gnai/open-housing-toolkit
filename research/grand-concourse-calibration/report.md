# 425 Grand Concourse real project calibration

The playbook recovered several conditions behind a real housing pursuit, but it did not reconstruct the complete design or financing. The strongest lesson is that **density depends on a specific legal and physical pathway**. The completed building's home count alone is a poor benchmark.

This is a known-case reconstruction, not a blind backtest. We already knew the outcomes; later documents were also re-read before committing the early assessment. Input facts have only 2015 citations, but the analyst's knowledge was not isolated. The protocol and early assessment have separate Git checkpoints; those protect the record from later editing, not from prior knowledge. No predictive accuracy or analyst time saving is claimed.

## What the early packet supports

The [June 2015 HPD RFP and two addenda](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/zip/425-grand-concourse-rfp.zip) describe a City-owned former school site of approximately 30,049 sf, existing C4-4 zoning, and a proposed C6-3 branch. They require mixed-income affordable housing and direct active ground-floor uses. Surveys, residual demolition, grading and environmental mitigation are developer responsibilities. Addendum 2 explicitly recognizes higher tower construction costs and uncertainty about the subway route.

The assessment preserves 29 exact PDF-page quotations, selected addendum refinements, a conditional pursuit judgment and four diligence actions with stop conditions. Applicant development and NYC management thresholds are **70 residential units within seven years**. Actual team credentials, cash capacity, historical program terms, floor-plan fit and committed financing remain unknown. A nominal $1 land price also carries an enforcement note and conditions.

The proposed first check is team eligibility, followed by coordinated architect/engineer and capital-stack scoping. Its cost and speed have not been measured; no optimal ordering is established. The 157-page RFP includes appendices and forms; this run is not an exhaustive compliance inventory or spreadsheet audit.

CPC action alone does not establish every final legal effective date or the cause of the story-count difference.

## Useful traps for the pipeline

**Home counts change across proposal versions.** HPD's April 2016 selected plan had 241 homes; the 2022 opening reports 277, an increase of 36. This is a program-version difference, not a forecast error. It cannot validate our unrelated Edward L Grant lot illustration of 271 homes.

**Exceptions prevent naive compliance conclusions.** HDC reports 45 studios among 277 apartments, or 16.25%, above the RFP's base 15% limit. Addendum 1 allowed supportive-housing flexibility. The approved final program and applicable exception are missing, so the correct flag is “needs program evidence,” not “noncompliant.” The 46 three-bedroom apartments represent 16.61%. The RFP also literally says “thirty (35) years”; retain the ambiguity for clarification.

**Area and cost labels matter.** The RFP's approximate 30,049 sf lot differs from CPC's 29,161 sf by 888 sf; the cause is unresolved. CPC itself gives approximate project totals of 275,711 sf and 275,771 sf in different passages, while the listed use areas total 276,071 sf. These are source discrepancies, not permission to choose a convenient denominator. [NYSERDA](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/About/Publications/Featured-Case-Studies/Buildings-of-Excellence-425-Grand-Concourse) reports 310,758 gross sf, 246,198 conditioned sf and a $121,942,998 figure labeled Project Cost. Conditioned area is not apartment net area. HDC reports a $178 million development. Those cost scopes and price vintages are not reconciled; no like-for-like cost error or universal construction rate can be derived.

**Required capital includes the baseline.** The prior Edward L Grant illustration needs $45,965,346.70 baseline non-debt capital plus $45,651,124.20 additional capital: $91,616,470.90 total modeled non-debt capital before excluded costs. Closing only the extra gap does not fund a project. This arithmetic correction is not 425 underwriting; the real affordable project uses several debt, subsidy and equity categories rather than a generic commercial loan assumption.

## What was tested and what remains

Offline validation resolves all 42 retained quotations, checks input/reference boundaries and file hashes, and recomputes the explicit arithmetic. Seven test methods challenge quote changes, reference leakage, changed-source quotations, disappearing baseline capital, cost-scope compatibility, studio exceptions and absent building layouts. These controls catch known interpretation failures. They do not discover every omitted requirement or establish professional correctness.

Next, turn these interpretation boundaries into the shared IR and pipeline, then apply the revised tool to a genuinely withheld case. Freeze an assessment before revealing later evidence and include a documented delayed, changed or unsuccessful case. A housing practitioner still needs to assess coverage, action sequencing and usefulness; a fresh AI reviewer cannot substitute for that acceptance.
