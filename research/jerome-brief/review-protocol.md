# Independent advisor review protocol

Please review the 1337 Jerome Avenue brief independently before reading the other advisor’s response. This tests whether an open housing playbook produces useful, traceable work. It does not ask you to approve a development, endorse a bidder or act for HPD.

Suggested first pass: 30–45 minutes. Record actual time if measured; otherwise leave null. Read the summary/scenarios and zoning issue, affordability/program discussion and requirements, then inspect at least three consequential claims in claim-trace.md against their original sources. Follow with a short conversation focused on the largest decision-changing gap.

Use one A/B form per advisor. Enter name, expertise, date, version fingerprint and whether this was an independent first pass. Answer every question, including “outside my expertise” where appropriate. Disposition choices: accept_for_second_site, revise or reject. Acceptance is about this research workflow, not legal feasibility.

1. **next_decision** — What concrete next decision does this packet let you make, and which role would make it?

2. **usefulness** — Would you use it in your workflow? Name the artifact it would replace or improve and the most useful section.

3. **fatal_errors** — Identify each material error or unsupported claim by claim ID; write none if none found. State consequence and correction.

4. **zoning** — Which rule branches explain the 175/185-foot concepts given PLUTO MIH flags and §§141-23/141-25? What evidence resolves applicability?

5. **approval** — Is the ELURP concept eligible? Which criteria, public-land disposition and environmental steps must be checked?

6. **units** — Are the ~48/~60 estimates reasonable with the actual core, setbacks, amenity area and unit mix? What makes them fail?

7. **buyers** — Which household sizes/incomes can buy and remain given savings, mortgage access, maintenance, reserves and resale restrictions?

8. **finance** — What would an actual Open Door sources/uses and sales/maintenance model require? Which missing input changes the recommendation most?

9. **environment** — What environmental investigation/pathway is needed for the former pest-control facility and elevated-rail frontage?

10. **community** — Which published local priorities are needed? Is the packet keeping uncollected community views separate from agency goals?

11. **control** — What current title, occupancy, site-control or sponsor evidence is required before treating this as executable?

12. **provenance** — Can you follow a consequential claim to the original source passage and identify assumptions? Name a broken or unclear trace.

13. **workflow** — Using your usual approach as baseline, which work remains manual and which checks would you automate? Give measured time if available; otherwise label estimate.

14. **acceptance** — What exact correction or additional evidence would make you accept this playbook for a second site?

15. **signoff** — Which conclusions would you refuse to sign, and which parts lie outside your expertise?

## Reconcile after the independent reviews

Record each material issue by claim/requirement ID, evidence, consequence, proposed correction, owner and disposition. Preserve disagreements rather than averaging them into a score. Ask a relevant professional to decide disagreements outside either advisor’s expertise. Changes to evidence, assumptions or interpretation create a new packet fingerprint and reset review validity.

For a second-site pilot, require both reviewers to identify a useful next decision, complete their scoped review, and accept the corrected workflow with no unresolved material issues. Compare against a redacted customary memo if they can supply one. Measure completeness, material-error count, trace success, work/time saved and remaining manual work; do not claim speed gains without a baseline.

The validator checks form completeness and fingerprint binding. It does not authenticate reviewer identity or enforce that they did not communicate. The project owner must confirm attribution and independence. Receiving a form is not a substitute for professional scope-specific signoff.
