## Open Door requirements that change the analysis

The linked Open Door term sheet is dated June 30, 2025. It describes homeownership, not a rental debt-coverage model. The property must be HDFC-owned during construction. Cooperative projects have a minimum 40-year regulatory agreement, owner-occupancy and resale restrictions, operating/replacement reserves, a minimum 10% resale flip tax to the co-op, an approved monitor/property manager and minimum 2% annual maintenance increases. [hdfc_ownership](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/services/open-door-term-sheet.pdf) (PDF page 1 Eligible Borrowers); [coop_regulatory_years](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/services/open-door-term-sheet.pdf) (PDF page 3 Regulatory Requirements).

| Underwritten AMI tier | Published maximum subsidy per home |
|---|---:|
| Up to 80% | $445,000 |
| 81–90% | $430,000 |
| 91–100% | $365,000 |
| 101–120% | $355,000 |

These are contingent maxima, not commitments or a project budget. The sheet conditions them on AHOP/AHC availability, market conditions and HPD’s determination. We have not verified funding availability or the eventual Jerome unit mix. [Open Door table](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/services/open-door-term-sheet.pdf) (PDF page 1 table); [subsidy_contingency](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/services/open-door-term-sheet.pdf) (PDF page 1).

Prices are set jointly with HPD at levels affordable to households earning 70–120% AMI or as dictated by market, with at least a 10% discount to market. The sheet caps total housing costs at 33% of annual income and uses maintenance, shareholder-paid utilities and projected mortgage payments, with a 30-year mortgage, 5% down payment and current fixed rates. This is a rule to apply, not a verified price. [Sales price rules](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/services/open-door-term-sheet.pdf) (PDF page 1 Sales Prices).

## What would make the concepts fail

- The actual surveyed geometry, MIH/subdistrict applicability, setbacks, elevated-rail rules, core/elevators or ground-floor uses support fewer units or a different envelope.
- The extra homes require costs or funding that cannot be supported by restricted sales, subsidy availability, reserves and maintenance.
- Prospective buyers meet income limits but cannot fund down payment/closing costs, obtain a mortgage or sustain escalating maintenance.
- Community needs require a different amenity or housing mix, or the final RFP/qualified-developer conditions differ from this reconstruction.
- Site control, occupancy, contamination, rail noise/air or infrastructure obligations change the design, costs or schedule.

These are analyst risk hypotheses, not findings about this property.

## Financial model contract

The machine-readable `finance-inputs.json` is a completeness contract, with missing inputs represented as null rather than zero. It asks for acquisition/disposition/land debt, gross and zoning areas, hard/soft/demolition/remediation costs, contingency, financing/interest/carry, fees, construction reserves, tax exemption and timing, public/private sources, unit/income mix, restricted sale proceeds, maintenance/utility/reserve costs and buyer financing/access. There is no invented cost estimate, funding gap or projected sales price.

Keep project sources/uses separate from buyer financing and ongoing co-op operations. A project can balance financially while excluding its intended buyers. A nominal $1 public-land disposition does not erase land debt, restrictions or transaction costs.
