# Separate AI assumptions review

Reviewed 7 October 2026. I am a fresh-context AI agent who did not build this experiment, **not an unfamiliar human housing expert**. This review does not supply practitioner acceptance, professional clearance, or the pending human reproduction/challenge required by the protocol. I reviewed the README, spec, report, protocol, baseline graph, conclusion engine, original `probe.json`, reused scenario arithmetic, and exact cached source bodies/receipts. I did not change existing code, configuration, frozen outputs, specification, or review templates.

My disposition: the modest instruction to investigate occupancy before committing to design remains sensible, but **the evidence does not establish occupancy as the best first investigation**. A material housing-related signal already in the cached DOB source was omitted from the graph. The geometry and finance are internally consistent examples, with insufficient support to interpret them as developable homes, achievable rents, or required public subsidy. Existing caveats are valuable and should remain visible whenever numbers are reused.

## Findings by materiality

### High — a real discordant housing signal is outside the selected historical evidence

`cache/dob_bis_2028710002.body`, zero-based **row 7**, records job `200774199`, document `02`, pre-filing date `02/13/2003`, `job_status_descrp="SIGNED OFF"`, signoff `10/18/2004`, and **`proposed_dwelling_units="9"`**. Its description concerns plumbing/mechanical modifications and says no change in use, egress, or occupancy. Row 8 is document `01` of the same job. By contrast, `spec.json` selects only historical rows 1, 3, and 11, and `conclusion_engine.py` imports only description/date/job/document fields from those rows. This leaves the nine-unit field outside `outputs/baseline.json` and the displayed investigation narrative.

This is **not** proof that nine homes exist now, that this field was correctly entered, or that PLUTO's `unitsres=0` is wrong. A historical proposed count is semantically different from a current recorded unit count; it is not a same-field/version contradiction. It is, however, directly relevant evidence against relying on selected commercial-use passages as a complete account of housing risk. The cached receipt authenticates the source bytes, not this field's substantive accuracy. Synthetic contradictory-unit controls do not test discovery of this real heterogeneous signal.

Concrete reconciliation: retrieve both job documents and approved plans, identify the relevant BIN/address and building portions, review the certificate of occupancy or alternative lawful-use record and its history, explain the proposed-unit field, then verify present use through appropriate current records and site investigation. Preserve both assertions and their dates until reconciled; do not overwrite zero with nine. A useful falsification test is whether a reviewer given the whole source inventory independently surfaces this signal without being told the selected rows or expected disposition.

### High — occupancy-first is a fixed policy, not an evaluated ordering of investigations

`spec.json` pins occupancy-first priority; `conclusion_engine.py` chooses the disposition primarily from conflicts, missing selected passages, and the sign of recorded residential units. Finance and environmental facts are graph dependencies but cannot select title-first, owner-first, or environmental-first work. All gates remain unknown. Consequently, graph connection does not establish that each dependency actually informs task ordering. Cost sensitivities retaining occupancy-first demonstrate implementation of the policy, not policy superiority.

For a researcher preparing a challenge pack, a cheap current-use check may be worthwhile. For a purchaser, a unwilling owner, restrictive title/zoning-lot agreement, active competing development, or environmental impediment could make that work premature. The relevant decision is how to spend limited diligence time before design; no cost, lead-time, abandonment likelihood, or information-value comparison is supplied. `probe.json` also calls the private ownership label and area proxy a justification for a lead, although an owner label supplies neither control nor willingness.

Evidence request: specify the actor, available budget, permitted next actions, and objective; compare current-use verification, ownership/control and title checks, zoning analysis, and environmental status review on cost, timing and whether the result would stop the project. A practitioner could falsify the ordering by identifying a documented inexpensive stop condition that should be checked first. No change in a readiness label can substitute for that evidence. The present conclusion can survive as one reasonable diligence task without surviving as the best first task.

### High for any capacity use — 271 units is an allocation formula, not a fit test

`pipeline.py:scenario` multiplies 59,050 sf by recorded residential FAR 7.52 and an assumed 0.65, then treats zoning-area proxy and gross area as equal, allocates 80 percent to net residential area, divides by an 850 sf mean unit, and floors the result. Nothing fits a massing to the site or a unit plan to a floor. The original row also records `irrlotcode=true`, `spdist1="J"`, `overlay1="C2-4"`, and MIH options 1 and 3. These are material leads for applicability review; they are not all carried as explicit constraints in the conclusion graph.

The 55 percent footprint assumption creates an illustrative nine-storey/90-foot calculation in the original scenario arithmetic, but footprint, floors and height are outside the conclusion's retained dependency closure because displayed quantities do not depend on them. Changing coverage therefore need not change 271 units even if an actual buildable envelope could no longer contain that allocation. Applying only 65 percent of a tabular FAR does not make the result a conservative feasible lower bound.

`cache/pluto_dictionary.txt`, Residential FAR section around line 1900, describes a district-derived reference and ideal-condition limitations. This cached dictionary is **22v1**, while the row is **26v2**: its definitions are contextual support, not proof that all current rules and fields match. The cached `jerome_zoning.txt` contains street-wall, ground-floor, height and setback provisions whose applicability needs mapped subarea/frontage analysis. I have not determined which specific clauses legally bind this parcel.

Falsification/evidence request: establish the tax-lot versus zoning-lot boundary and development-rights ownership; obtain a survey and mapped zoning/special-district/MIH analysis effective at the chosen date; test yards, setbacks, daylight/open space, ground-floor uses, access, egress, accessibility, servicing, utilities, unit mix and structural layout in an actual envelope. Account explicitly for retained commercial/community uses or demolition and relocation. Compare a separate architect's area schedule with the formula, including gross-to-zoning conversion and nonresidential allocations. Failure to fit the assumed area invalidates the numerical concept even if occupancy investigation remains useful.

### High for finance use — gap closure leaves a large unexplained capital requirement

The baseline arithmetic is consistent with its declared inputs, but `additional_non_debt_capital=max(0, illustrative_loan-rent_supported_debt)` measures only the extra capital needed **above** an assumed 35 percent non-debt contribution. At baseline, modeled cost is $131.330 million, the assumed loan $85.364 million, and rent-supported debt $39.713 million. Baseline non-debt capital is therefore **$45.965 million**, plus the displayed **$45.651 million** additional amount: approximately **$91.616 million total non-debt capital**, before excluded costs. `baseline_non_debt_capital` is calculated in `pipeline.py` but omitted from the retained baseline graph and display.

Under the $140/sf control, the extra gap becomes zero, but roughly **$18.386 million** of assumed baseline non-debt capital still has to come from somewhere. The report carefully calls this a modeled additional gap, yet reuse of the Boolean `finance.model_gap` or the phrase “gap closes” could suggest the entire financing problem has closed. It has not. There is no demonstrated equity investor, subsidy program, source-and-use balance, or return requirement.

Other material dependencies remain untested: taxes/abatement eligibility, utility allowances, rent and bedroom mix, regulatory affordability bands, fixed operating costs, reserves, insurance, lease-up, construction draw interest, land, remediation, relocation and demolition. A constant 35 percent expense ratio makes expenses fall when rents fall, although many costs do not; it can overstate debt support at low rents. Subsidized permanent debt, tax-credit equity or grants could also make the market-rate loan illustration an unsuitable affordability benchmark. These caveats cut in different directions, so $3,869 is neither a demonstrated market rent nor an all-in feasibility threshold.

Evidence request: a dated unit-level rent/utility allowance schedule, local fixed and variable operating budget, full sources-and-uses, realistic construction/permanent terms and verified program eligibility. State both baseline and additional non-debt requirements. Reject any claim of finance closure if a required capital source is uncommitted. Stress correlated changes, such as smaller units with changed rent mix and per-unit expenses, rather than just one favorable cost input.

### Medium — zero residential units is not a comprehensive displacement measure

The snapshot records zero residential units and 26,000 sf retail area. The selected filings date to 2002, 2004 and 2015; the school-to-hall application was merely processed in the cached status, while the laundromat work was signed off and the medical awning had a permit. These distinctions support different strengths of historical interpretation. An awning reading a name does not establish a clinic operation. The generated wording mostly respects that limit.

The 22v1 dictionary's UnitsRes definition also excludes hotels/motels, nursing homes and SROs from that unit count. Apply version caution, but the general semantic problem remains: zero is not absence of people, vulnerable residents or social-service displacement. The church/school/hall and commercial signals could imply important community-service impacts even without homes. The original ranking explicitly disclaims a welfare ranking; it should not be repackaged as low-displacement potential.

Evidence request: identify actual users, employees and services, present leases and tenure protections, informal or unrecorded residential use, relocation feasibility and community consequences. Disaggregate verified residential and nonresidential impacts. Such findings could falsify a preference for this parcel while leaving recorded zero factually intact.

### Medium — transit proximity supplies no usable-access conclusion

The baseline traces a roughly 434-metre haversine distance from a PLUTO lot coordinate to station 387's published coordinate and route 4. This is correctly labeled straight-line proximity. Neither coordinate is demonstrated to be the relevant door/entrance; route labels are not scheduled service, accessible entrance availability, journey reliability or employment reach. The original 650-metre screen is a preference, not a validated catchment.

Evidence request: route from the usable parcel entrance to an operating station entrance, verify crossings, grade, walking conditions and accessibility, and measure door-to-door job access at relevant times with dated service data. A walk that is materially longer, unsafe, inaccessible for the intended users or poorly connected to jobs falsifies the access proxy, not the cached station coordinate. No current walk, accessibility or journey check was performed here.

### Medium — source integrity and control success do not show inventory completeness

I accept byte replay and exact locators as useful engineering evidence. The four-family/73-claim graph and eleven expected control results do not establish semantic recall, an exhaustive gate inventory, or completeness of the site's history. The engine handles synthetic competing assertions only for supported parcel fields, and holds on different dataset versions without resolving authority or observation dates. A dataset release date is not necessarily the date a use changed. Requiring every selected old historical passage to release any investigation judgment may also be unnecessarily brittle: losing a 2004 sign passage need not eliminate the reason to investigate present occupancy.

Test both false negatives and false holds using heterogeneous, dated evidence and incomplete packets. Ask an independent reviewer to find material constraints in full sources before seeing the graph. Evaluate supported reason-giving and appropriately bounded abstention; do not score “hold” as automatically correct or reward every unknown being listed.

## Retrospective testing without outcome matching

1. Choose the test question and case eligibility before inspecting outcomes. Use an actual project with recoverable contemporaneous pre-design/pre-award evidence, including unsuccessful, delayed or changed projects where available. Do not pick only a celebrated completed project that resembles the formula. Record what the analyst already knows; contamination cannot be repaired by calling a known case blind.
2. Freeze an as-of input packet, source-access rules, identities/aliases, assumptions and evaluation criteria before generating recommendations. Keep later design, financing and delivery records in a separate reveal packet. Today's PLUTO row or post-construction certificate cannot masquerade as an early input. When contemporaneous evidence cannot be recovered, classify that dimension untestable.
3. Obtain a contemporaneous or independently authored comparator for diligence sequencing: what ownership/control, current-use, environmental and entitlement questions should have been asked next, why, at what approximate cost, and what answers would change the decision. A human expert should record this before seeing the model output. Score decision relevance and missed stop conditions, not literal agreement with “occupancy first.”
4. Freeze independently grounded numerical ranges and explicit unknowns for achievable area, unit mix, nonresidential space and financing. After reveal, explain deviations against contemporaneous approved design and underwriting, distinguishing prediction error from later scope/rule/market changes. Compare similar gross-area definitions and dollar years. A delivered unit count alone neither proves nor disproves the original geometry assumption.
5. Test source interpretation on the full packet: did the system surface conflicting residential signals, distinguish filing from completion, preserve dates, and request the right reconciliation? Include adverse evidence, real aliases and source gaps. Measure material missed signals and unwarranted clearances alongside justified holds. This targets the row-7 omission rather than rewarding synthetic field conflicts the author already expected.
6. Freeze failure criteria, such as missed known occupants, an unsupported claim of feasible area, omission of an available decisive title restriction, or finance closure despite unexplained required capital. Score each dimension separately. Do not retroactively waive eligibility, retune mean unit size/FAR share to match delivered units, change the rubric after reveal, or count a correct guess made from leaked outcomes as success.
7. Separate a frozen first evaluation from later learning. Put any improved assumptions/policy and source-reconciliation rules in a new version, retain the original failure record, and evaluate the changes on another untouched case. One successful retrospective cannot establish generalization or practitioner usefulness; engineering controls remain a different evidence category.

The review supports continuing a clearly provisional research challenge, with the omitted DOB housing signal made visible in any subsequent interpretation. It does not support acquisition, design, feasibility, optimal priority, or expert-acceptance claims.
