# 97 West 169th Street document assessment results

The first controlled assessment is complete. It found consequential parcel, physical, legal and financing constraints, but missed an applicant-eligibility gate that could stop a pursuit before expensive design work. This is a useful failure to build against. It supports continuing the document-assessment probe; it does not establish practitioner usefulness, time savings or development feasibility.

## What was tested

The question was whether a fresh analyst context, given only the December 2018 HPD solicitation and three pre-deadline addenda, could produce a traceable initial assessment: site identity, possible pathways, invalidating constraints, missing evidence and next actions. The declared cutoff was April 4, 2019. This was not a unit-count prediction, a full RFP response, a zoning calculation or an econometric analysis.

The curator had already seen the selected outcome. The analyst received only the input-folder task, manifest and four PDF/text pairs, without curator notes, rubric, archive narrative or later announcement. Its declared access log lists those files and reports no recalled outcome. This was an instruction boundary with a fresh context, not an operating-system sandbox or proof against prior model knowledge. Map graphics were not visually reviewed.

The rubric and input hashes were committed before the analyst started. The original assessment, claims and access log were then frozen with hashes and committed before the curator opened the later reference during this run. Exact historical byte equivalence remains uncertified; no historical parcel/zoning/market datasets were added. The input preparation manifest remains unchanged as a historical receipt; the separate run artifacts record completion.

## What the assessment did well

- **Caught conflicting parcel identity.** RFP page 8 names Block 2519, Lots 27/32; page 9 says Block 2509. Addendum 1 supports 2519 as the working identity. The assessment requests verification instead of silently joining a preferred number.
- **Kept permission separate from feasibility.** It identified R7-1/AIRS and senior-affordable program requirements, while leaving legal FAR, building envelope and unit yield unknown. Reported parkland legislation did not become proof of title or completed approvals.
- **Exposed terrain and survey limits.** It distinguishes a respondent’s rock/outcropping concern from an agency geotechnical finding. Public contours and an equipment-free visit did not become a surveyed envelope.
- **Preserved financing branches.** It used the stated up-to SARA caps and 4%/9% evidence requirements without turning them into funding commitments. It retained prevailing-wage, service funding, nonresidential cost and tax-benefit uncertainties.
- **Produced next actions.** Its table names counsel, surveyor, engineers, architect, environmental consultant, finance lead and project manager, with evidence to obtain and conditions that could change or stop a concept.

These observations come from curator review of the frozen assessment and its 24 claim statements against cited document context. They are not independent professional acceptance. The precommitted criterion dispositions are retained in evaluation.json, without an aggregate success score.

## The material miss

The assessment omitted HPD’s threshold-versus-competitive review framework and material applicant-qualification checks. RFP pages 17–19 make completeness, team experience, finance and zoning obligations part of the threshold review. Page 19 requires, among other conditions:

- At least one principal with a successfully completed new-construction or substantial-rehabilitation mixed-use project of at least 60 residential units within seven years.
- Qualifying experience managing at least 60 residential units in New York City within seven years, with the ownership/management conditions stated there.

The practitioner needs to know whether its team can satisfy these conditions or must change before spending on the site. A sound physical assessment does not answer “Can we credibly pursue this solicitation?” The next-action table failed to put that decision first. The team’s actual credentials are absent, so the correct gate would be **unknown, evidence required**, rather than pass or fail.

The original output stays unchanged. This omission is recorded in evaluation.json. A separate erratum also records the curator’s precommitted contour-page locator error: Addendum 2 page 2, not page 3. No criterion was changed after output.

## Engineering checks and limits

Technical validation passes: 24 typed claims and 82 quotations matched to their stated PDF pages; unchanged input/rubric/output hashes; declared-read allowlist; dependency integrity. Seven rejection/acceptance tests cover wrong-page quotes, invented quotes, unallowlisted sources, dependency cycles, untraced calculations and frozen-output mutation. The three existing date-boundary tests also pass.

Those checks verify provenance mechanics. They do not prove that every quote entails its claim, that all consequential prose is present in the ledger, or that no important requirement was missed. The applicant-eligibility omission is direct evidence of that limitation.

The analyst pass took roughly 14 minutes from task dispatch to final artifact freeze, including orchestration and provenance work. That is an approximate run duration, not a latency benchmark or a measure of human time saved. No comparable baseline or advisor review was run.

## Next slice

Represent applicant eligibility and submission requirements in the IR as explicit **pass / unknown / fail** gates with source passage, required evidence, responsible role and downstream decision. Unknown team credentials should block claims of readiness to submit and surface a request for credentials or a qualified partner; they should not prevent low-cost exploration.

Test this addition on another held-out solicitation with the same freeze-before-reveal process. Measure missed material requirements and unsupported gate promotions. Do not tune it until this case matches the selected project. A practitioner review remains necessary to judge whether the pack changes an actual next decision and reduces rework.
