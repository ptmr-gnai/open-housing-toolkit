# Public case for a retrospective housing playbook test

**Recommend 97 West 169th Street in Highbridge, Bronx, for the first document-based reconstruction.** We retrieved the original December 2018 HPD RFP, all three 2019 addenda, and a later agency developer-selection announcement. This is a real completed selection process; construction completion has not been verified. A full submitted proposal and private underwriting were not found.

The case is ready for a bounded document exercise. It is not yet a certified historical back-test or a test of practitioner usefulness. Exact historical byte equivalence and historical parcel/zoning/market inputs remain unverified; advisors are still needed for the usefulness question.

## What we searched

We followed HPD’s [designated-project archive](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/designated-rfp-projects.page) to its linked JavaScript and [CSV](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/data/rfp-rfq-rfei-designated.csv). The page is a shell until its records load. The retrieved CSV has 101 entries, 16 explicitly labeled Bronx. It is an archive of designated requests, not an exhaustive census of housing projects or failed proposals.

We inspected four Bronx candidates with original solicitations and later reference material. Search-engine discovery was attempted, but the Bing responses did not reliably reflect the full queries; substantive claims below come from agency documents. We retained 31 request receipts, including failures, with response hashes and exact URLs.

Selection criteria were Bronx location, manageable initial-assessment scope, available dated solicitation/addenda, meaningful constraints, and a dated later reference. Selection was not based on matching a particular unit count.

## Why this is an informative test

These are curator observations, intended for designing the evaluation. Keep this section out of the analyst input context.

- **Identity contradictions:** RFP page 8 identifies Block 2519, Lots 27 and 32; page 9 says Block 2509. Addendum 1 page 2 again identifies Block 2519. A credible assessment should report and reconcile the discrepancy, rather than silently accepting a convenient join.
- **Land-use history:** RFP page 8 says the site was mapped as parkland and describes 2017 legislation authorizing discontinuance for affordable housing. It separately identifies disposition/UDAAP and public-approval steps. An assessor should preserve these distinctions and request legal verification of the actual requirements.
- **Physical constraints:** Addendum 1 page 2 discusses extreme elevation changes, rock outcroppings, absence of an HPD-provided survey and limits of the arranged site visit. Public contours are useful but not a surveyed building envelope or geotechnical investigation.
- **Program-specific capacity:** RFP page 10 identifies R7-1 and additional floor area pursuant to Affordable Independent Residences for Seniors. The analysis must use the applicable historical rules and qualification conditions, rather than importing current City of Yes zoning.
- **Finance branches:** RFP page 21 distinguishes SARA subsidies for 4% bond/LIHTC and 9% tax-credit scenarios; Addendum 2 allows either if realistic and feasible and requests supporting evidence for the 9% route. A larger subsidy ceiling is not evidence that a competitive award will be obtained.
- **Nonresidential space:** RFP pages 9 and 21 require commercial/community-facility costs to be supported by income or developer equity. Desirable amenities are not financially free.
- **Proposal evaluation:** RFP page 17 separates threshold from competitive review, including feasibility, financing capacity, zoning compliance, team qualifications and completeness. The playbook should expose those obligations rather than optimize only housing yield.

These challenges can be checked against supplied documents without an advisor. Whether the resulting assessment is professionally correct or useful still needs scoped expert review.

## The first experiment we can run without advisors

**Hypothesis:** Given only a dated RFP and clarifying addenda, the playbook can produce an initial site assessment that exposes consequential constraints, distinguishes requirements from assumptions, and identifies evidence needed for the next decision.

This is narrower than the original hypothesis about practitioner usefulness and time saved. We cannot measure those without practitioners and a comparable baseline.

1. Freeze the input-only packet and a rubric before the analyst run. Permit only its four PDFs and source text; exclude later reference material and unrestricted web browsing.
2. Produce the existing-condition assessment, pathways worth investigating, constraints that could kill them, missing evidence and next actions. Require document/page citations for consequential claims and declared inputs for calculations.
3. Freeze the assessment before reference reveal. Score documentary accuracy, contradiction handling, completeness, calibration of unknowns and traceability. Do not score a unit-count match, selected-developer match or similarity of renderings.
4. Reveal the later selection summary and compare public program choices and constraints. Record both convergence and reasonable divergence. Do not treat promotional agency claims as verified performance or the selected design as the only feasible choice.
5. Record corrections and expert questions. Reserve usefulness, time savings, legal/architectural accuracy, private underwriting, construction delivery and resident outcomes for later evidence and professional review.

The prepared task does not run an analysis or grade itself. The three date-boundary tests reject post-cutoff issue dates and the observed post-cutoff PDF-metadata case. The input validator checks PDF/text hashes, date evidence on covers, file allowlisting and honest exposure/certification flags. Document integrity passes; certified historical back-test readiness remains blocked.

## Retention and reproduction

Original selected input PDFs and extracted text are tracked in `inputs/`, with exact hashes and ZIP-member lineage. Search receipts, parsed discovery text, archive records, PDF metadata, selection criteria and source acquisition scripts are tracked. Large discovery bodies and duplicate alternative extracts are retained locally outside Git; the acquisition ledger says so. Their URLs and hashes support a future fresh acquisition, not guaranteed byte-identical replay from the repository.

Run `python research/benchmark-case-search/validate_inputs.py` and `python -m unittest discover -s research/benchmark-case-search/tests -v`. `prepare_case.py` recreates the input packet from the locally retained original archive/extracts; it requires those discovery artifacts. The supplied input ZIP is independently usable for the analyst task. Upstream agency documents retain their own terms; original code and documentation follow the repository’s licenses.
