Q1: Can HPD provide a survey given the topography of the Site is so challenging?
A1: HPD cannot provide a survey of the Site. Given the topography of the Site, additional information on
elevation and site boundaries is provided in part B of this Addendum.

Q2: The most important information is topological information because of the extreme elevation
changes and rock outcroppings. It’s difficult to plan a responsive building without knowing more
about the topo information.
A2: Given the topography of the Site, additional information on elevation and site boundaries is provided
in part B of this Addendum.

The contour maps in part B of this Addendum were made using a planimetric basemap layer containing
citywide 2-foot contour line features and is hosted on the NYC Open Data website at this link:
https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Contours/3cdm-p29e.

The New York City Department of Finance (DOF) Digital Tax Map is available at this link:
http://maps.nyc.gov/taxmap/. This map, along with information from DOF Automated City Register
Information System (ACRIS) (https://acrisweb.csc.nycnet/cp/) for Bronx Block 2519, Lots 27 and 32 and
surrounding tax parcels provides information about the boundaries of the Development Site.

Q5: Can you describe the post-selection process, who is doing it, and how long will it take?
A5: The Site is City-owned property and it will have to go through the Uniform Land Use Review
Procedure (ULURP) process that gives the City the authority to dispose of the property. After a selection
is made, HPD will work with the selected development team to prepare for ULURP and put together the
ULURP package. Pre-ULURP, or the preparation period before ULURP begins, includes working with the
architect, doing an environmental study, working with HPD’s Bronx Planning, Building and Land
Development Services (BLDS), and Special Needs Housing units, and with the New York City
Department of City Planning (DCP). Pre-ULURP may take up to a year or more. At the point at which the
application is considered complete and satisfactory to DCP, the application gets submitted to DCP and is
certified. Certification is the official start of ULURP.

ULURP itself will take approximately seven months. HPD will be the applicant on the ULURP. HPD will be
responsible for most of the write up of the application and will ask the development team to provide
drawings, a zoning analysis, and other required exhibits. The Community Board will hold a hearing to

                                                   2
review the ULURP application and will give an advisory opinion followed by a hearing given by the
Borough President and an advisory opinion from the Borough President. Then the application will go to
the City Planning Commission and the City Council and finish with a Mayoral Hearing. HPD will shepherd
the team through the process, with coordination and prep calls and meetings for each milestone. From
the time of selection, the pre-ULURP and ULURP process will take about a year and half to two years.

Q6: What is the status of the park that is planned on the north side of the block?
A6: The New York City Department of Parks and Recreation (DPR) hosted a scoping for the design of
Corporal Fischer Park in January 2019 with the community. DPR is now developing the design of the park
and construction is anticipated to begin in 2021.

Q7: Is the park fully funded?
A7: Yes, Corporal Fischer Park is fully funded.

Q8: Given where the park is at in the planning process, will the park be available for construction
staging for the housing development?
A8: If all proposed staging activity terminates by spring 2021, DPR is open to reviewing proposals to use
the park for construction staging.

Q9: What is HPD’s policy on reaching out to City Council or community boards?
A9: Respondents are open to engaging with stakeholders as they see fit. HPD will assess and score only
on the submissions.

Q10: Given the City’s recent stance on modular construction, which has been really positive, are
there any special considerations here for a team that uses modular construction?
A10: There is no scoring preference for using modular construction.

Q15: When is the 2019 SARA Term Sheet coming out?
A15: Respondents to the RFP should adhere to the SARA term sheet that is dated November 13, 2014
and is currently on HPD’s website. Note that a subsidy amount of up to $125,000 per unit is allowed for
proposals assuming 9% Low Income Housing Tax Credits and up to $75,000 per unit is allowed for
proposals assuming 4% credits. HPD will provide further instructions if a new SARA term sheet is
                           th
released before the April 4 submission deadline.

Q16: If the new SARA term sheet comes out on April 2 and submissions are due on April 4, what
version of the term sheet should respondents use?
A16: Respondents to the RFP should adhere to the SARA term sheet that is dated November 13, 2014
and is currently on HPD’s website. Note that a subsidy amount of up to $125,000 per unit is allowed for
proposals assuming 9% Low Income Housing Tax Credits and up to $75,000 per unit is allowed for
proposals assuming 4% credits. HPD will provide further instructions if a new SARA term sheet is
                           th
released before the April 4 submission deadline.

Q17: If using NYC 15/15 rental subsidy, does the rental subsidy apply to only the homeless units
or could the rental subsidy apply to all the units and NYC 15/15 operating support only apply for
the homeless services?
A17: Respondents may replace the SARA program’s 30% general population homeless set-aside
requirement with a 30% supportive housing set-aside. Such projects may assume service and rental
assistance funding through the New York City 15/15 Supportive Housing Initiative or other available
funding sources. Rental assistance associated with NYC 15/15 or other supportive housing operating
funding should only be applied to the supportive units. Non-supportive units would receive project based
Section 8 vouchers.

Q18: Should proposals assume prevailing wage?
A18: Yes, the SARA term sheet assumes that project based Section 8 vouchers will be used, which
triggers Davis-Bacon prevailing wage requirements.

Q19: Does the supportive set aside have to be senior supportive or any supportive population?
A19: For the purposes of the RFP, assume units are restricted to residents 62 years and older.

Q20: Do the age restricted units have to be restricted to 55 or 62?
A20: For the purposes of the RFP, assume units are restricted to residents 62 years and older.

Q21: Are there any ADA requirements for the units?
A21: Submissions must comply with the accessibility requirements laid out in HPD’s Design Guidelines
for Multifamily New Construction and Senior Housing, which may include the Americans with Disabilities
Act.

Q22: How will special inspections be handled for this project? How are they handled on other HPD
projects?
A22: Special inspections are conducted during construction to verify that work was performed in
accordance with approved plans and specifications, and they must be performed by special inspectors on
behalf of a registered special inspection agency. Special inspections will not happen prior to construction
