# 730 Stanyan: how a public housing project is evaluated over time

Research date: November 9, 2026. **The useful method to reuse is a sequence of
bounded decisions supported by linked design, capital, operating and services
budgets.** Stanyan shows how an agency revisits those decisions as designs,
funding awards and costs change. It does not supply a universal cost rate,
optimal housing mix or transferable legal approval.

This is an analyst reconstruction of four public records. Later information was
known before this study began. No predictive accuracy, measured time saving,
professional endorsement, resident-outcome evaluation or complete Excel-model
reproduction is claimed. The main Bronx comparison is in
[bronx-comparison.md](bronx-comparison.md).

## 1. Begin with the decision and its stage

In 2020 the immediate request was a $4.5 million predevelopment loan. The report
separates that request from the possible permanent funding structure and operating
budget, which were presented to demonstrate plausibility rather than for final
approval. It assigns subsequent tasks: compare six- and eight-story schemes,
justify design fees, obtain commercial-tenant commitments, establish services
funding, and seek agency review of investors and lenders. These are conditions
for later advancement, not assertions that every project obligation has been
cleared. [S20 pp.15-24](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=15).

In 2022, design had advanced and the request supported applications for State
funding and tax-exempt financing. The report anticipates a return for final-gap
review after financing commitments. It revisits earlier conditions individually,
recording completion, work in progress and information still to be provided.
The six/eight-story comparison is recorded as completed with MOHCD choosing the
eight-story scheme. [S22 pp.2,22-24](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=22).

**Reusable method:** attach each conclusion to a question, date, budget version,
decision authority and next condition. A predevelopment approval cannot become
a construction approval merely because both use a sources-and-uses table.

## 2. Compare specific designs, with financing and delivery consequences

S20 describes a six-story, 120-unit budget. The sponsor's earlier eight-story
alternative had 152 units and an approximately $45.345 million MOHCD contribution,
versus approximately $34.325 million for the six-story scheme. The stated concern
was the local funding budget, even though an affordable-housing density-bonus
pathway could allow more height. [S20 pp.3-5](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=3).

By S22, the proposal is eight stories and 160 units, using Type IA concrete.
It is not the unchanged 152-unit option from 2020. The report ties the design to
an 83-foot density-bonus pathway and expected streamlined approval. Those are
historical agency interpretations, not independently established legal findings
or advice about current California law. [S22 pp.3,13,16](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=13).

The design review identifies curved facades, three street frontages, structural
form, daylight, unit stacking, environmental performance, sidewalk requirements
and glazing requirements as cost and design choices. It cites a March 2022 design
development set and April estimate. It follows successive construction estimates,
potential value engineering and owner contingency rather than multiplying a lot
area by a generic cost rate. Redesign added approximately four months and over
$750,000 in design/engineering fees according to the report.
[S22 pp.16-18,23](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=16).

S22's area schedule distinguishes 128,289 residential sf, 30,770 circulation sf,
16,000 common-area sf, 11,180 commercial sf and 7,500 back-of-house sf. These sum
to 193,739 sf. **That reconciliation does not establish that the residential
figure is apartment net area or that the drawings comply with every requirement.**
[S22 p.17](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=17).

The 40-home increase between the 2020 budget and 2022 proposal is a design-version
change. It is not a causal estimate of a zoning reform, nor evidence that adding
two stories always adds one-third more homes.

## 3. Reconstruct capital with explicit accounting boundaries

The following are reported budgets at different stages, not realized final costs.
The denominator includes the manager's unit. Dollar figures are nominal to each
document's assumptions; no inflation normalization is attempted.

| Budget | Homes | Reported development total | City land acquisition included? | Reported total / home, recomputed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 2020 preliminary permanent budget | 120 | $98,121,310 | No; $15.9m paid separately | $817,677.58 |
| 2022 preliminary gap budget | 160 | $150,040,047 | No; $15.9m paid separately | $937,750.29 |
| 2023 BLA development budget | 160 | $169,152,004 | Yes | $1,057,200.03 |

Sources: [S20 p.4](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=4),
[S22 pp.5-6](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=5),
[S23 pp.12-14](sources/board-packet-2023.pdf#page=12).

Subtracting the already-paid land amount from the 2023 total gives $153,252,004.
That is $3,211,957 above the 2022 land-excluding total. Comparing $150.04 million
directly with $169.15 million would mix scope change with other budget changes.
Even this land adjustment does not reconcile holding costs, fee definitions,
program changes, contingency releases or price dates.

The capital structures include several kinds of money: scheduled-payment private
debt, long-term residual-receipts public loans, tax-credit investor equity,
possible grants, deferred developer compensation and sponsor contributions.
Accrued interest is shown as a budget accounting item; it is not fresh cash
available to pay a contractor. Similarly, an increased developer fee partly
contributed back to the project is not entirely a cash distribution to a sponsor.

In S20, all listed permanent sources are explicitly uncommitted. In S22, much of
the funding remains requested or uncommitted. S22 also separates construction
bond financing from permanent sources and discusses a possible $1 million AHP
bridge. A construction loan repaid at conversion and its permanent takeout must
not both be counted as unrestricted permanent capital. A bridge and its expected
replacement are also not additive funding for the same use.
[S20 p.4](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=4),
[S22 pp.5-6,24](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=24).

S23 records the consequential funding change: the project did not obtain the
State MHP and infrastructure funding sought in that later application version.
The BLA attributes approximately $28.79 million of missing State resources to
replacement by MOHCD gap funding. This is not identical to S22's earlier $25.016
million combined assumption. S23's MOHCD loan is approximately $69.529 million,
including a $1 million AHP bridge; the separate land expenditure remains visible.
[S23 pp.12-14](sources/board-packet-2023.pdf#page=12).

**Reusable method:** distinguish proposed amount, commitment status, source,
restriction, timing, interest treatment and repayment waterfall. A balanced
proposed budget establishes arithmetic compatibility, not financing availability.

## 4. Model operating feasibility separately from construction funding

S25 identifies 160 homes: 32 PSH/LOSP homes, 32 additional voucher-supported homes,
95 lottery homes and one manager's unit. Nineteen NPLH-funded homes are a subset
of the 32 PSH homes, not a fifth additive group. The bedroom totals are 35 studios,
43 one-bedroom, 42 two-bedroom and 40 three-bedroom homes.
[S25 pp.2-4](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=2).

Its worksheet separates tenant rent, federal voucher assistance and local
operating subsidy. It allocates income and expenses between LOSP and non-LOSP
homes; these shares can be specific to a cost rather than simply 20%/80%.
For example, LOSP bears 75% of front-desk staffing expense in the narrative.
[S25 pp.5-7,14](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=5).

| LOSP | $568,307 | Full-year projection for the 32 PSH homes |

The ratio $1,272,978 / $1,046,907 is 1.21594, reproducing the displayed 1.22
after rounding. The worksheet is a budget, not a realized operating statement.
Its printed spreadsheet errors and apparent stale principal labels in the lower
waterfall are not treated as verified loan balances.

The memo evaluates a 20-year horizon, expense growth, income growth, reserves
and the modeled end of private debt payments. A high first-year coverage ratio
does not by itself establish long-run adequacy. S20 even asks for more debt to
reduce the gap burden, illustrating that maximizing coverage is not the sole
objective. [S20 p.24](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=24),
[S25 pp.6-7,15,17](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=6).

### Two narrow stress calculations

These are analyst-authored static perturbations of S25's reported Year 1 NOI,
holding all other items fixed. They are not agency scenarios, forecasts or a
substitute for rebuilding its LOSP allocation and cash-flow model.

- Remove the full-year $568,307 LOSP amount without replacement: coverage falls
  to approximately **0.673**, and cash flow after hard debt becomes **-$342,236**.
- Raise the $2,575,541 base operating expenses by 10%, with no rent or subsidy
  adjustment: coverage becomes approximately **0.970**.

These calculations demonstrate dependence on operating support. They do not
predict a subsidy cancellation or quantify the probability of financial failure.
They also do not assume the agency would leave subsidy unchanged after expenses
changed; that fixed-support assumption is deliberate for this diagnostic.

## 5. Keep services, affordability and access connected but distinct

S25 budgets **$395,976 a year** for PSH services through a separate HSH contract.
Another **$193,969** for services to lottery and voucher households is already
inside building operations. Adding both service figures to the building budget
would count the latter twice. Building expenses plus the separate PSH services
budget total $2,971,517 before reserves, ground rent, bond fees and hard debt;
that combined figure is a labeled scope bridge, not a single contracted budget.
[S25 pp.9-10,14](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=9).

PSH tenant payments are estimated using 12 family homes at $250/month and 20 TAY
homes at $125/month: $66,000/year. These are budget assumptions, not individual
eligibility determinations or verified future resident incomes. Access differs
by group: coordinated-entry referrals, Housing Authority referrals and the
DAHLIA lottery. TCAC and MOHCD AMI restrictions are reported in different columns;
the same percentage cannot be assumed to mean the same dollar threshold.
[S25 pp.3-4,6](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=3).

The LOSP schedule runs from August 2025 through December 2040. The first full-year
amount is $568,307, but five opening-year months produce a rounded $236,794
disbursement. The displayed calendar-year disbursements sum to $11,720,975.
The memo says annual appropriations and possible surplus adjustments still apply.
The visually inspected approval page has approval marks and signatures dated
May 2 and May 5; those do not establish every contract condition was fulfilled or
the amounts were paid. [S25 pp.1-2,11,16](sources/operating-subsidy-2025.pdf#page=11).

## 6. Evaluate community uses through delivery obligations

The reports preserve competing requests about senior housing, youth services,
building size, elevators and neighborhood facilities. The agency reports
program/legal concerns about a senior set-aside; that is a historical attributed
interpretation, not our own legal conclusion. A third elevator would have cost
and unit-count consequences according to S22. The proposed senior center did
not attract responses in two procurements and was reprogrammed as a community
center by S23. [S20 pp.5-6](sources/predevelopment-2020.pdf#page=5),
[S22 pp.14-15](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=14),
[S23 p.16](sources/board-packet-2023.pdf#page=16).

A community-space label is not evidence of a delivered service. Separate its
area, shell construction, tenant improvements, operator, procurement, lease,
operating funding and opening date. S22 carries no commercial revenue at that
stage; S23 anticipates some food-hall contribution, with tenant plans still
incomplete. Both distinguish shell funding from tenant-funded buildout.
[S22 pp.18-19](sources/preliminary-gap-2022.pdf#page=18),
[S23 pp.15-16](sources/board-packet-2023.pdf#page=15).

The records are useful evidence of agency reasoning and reported outreach, not
proof of consensus, representative preferences or causal community benefit.

## 7. Preserve source disagreements instead of manufacturing consistency

| Issue | What the sources show | Treatment here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area boundary in 2020 | 144,770 residential + 20,000 commercial does not sum to 184,770 total; difference is 20,000 sf (S20 p.2) | Preserve all labels; no invented shared-area category |
| Area changes in 2022 | Component schedule p.17 sums; p.4 separately says 11,145 commercial sf versus 11,180 on pp.3/17 | Version/definition reconciliation required; choose p.17 only for its labeled schedule |
| Capital table rounding | Source rows sum to reported totals +$1 in 2020, -$1 in 2022 and -$1 in 2023 | Retain each residual; do not edit the source numbers |
| 2022 reserve unit costs | $1,255,254 reserve total, but displayed per-unit/per-sf cells repeat developer-fee values (S22 p.5) | Recompute denominator-based amounts; do not copy the unit rates |
| Commercial areas in 2023 | Listed 3,685 + 2,324 + 3,126 + 1,852 sf = 10,987, versus stated 9,135 (S23 p.15) | Unresolved 1,852 sf discrepancy; no silent choice |
| 2025 PBV income escalation | p.6 narrative puts PBV tenant income at 1%; pp.6-7 notes group PBV with 2.5%; worksheet separates tenant rents and assistance | Preserve conflict and distinguish income streams; no complete 20-year replay claimed |
| 2025 PSH staffing | p.5 parenthesis says 2.5 FTE separately funded; pp.9-10 specify 2.0 FTE | Mark conflicting staffing text; use the explicitly labeled services budget for its own scope |
| 2025 worksheet arithmetic | Displayed revenue minus vacancy is $1 above reported EGI; reported EGI less deductions is $1 below reported NOI | Reproduce reported ratios and show the rounding residuals separately |
| Developer selection | S20 describes a tie; BLA's later review finds a tabulation issue and recommends obtaining underlying scores (S23 pp.16-17) | Record the challenge; do not adjudicate without scoring sheets |

These differ in materiality. A dollar rounding residual is not equivalent to
uncertain funding, staffing or several thousand square feet. Technical checks
must report the difference and its consequences rather than collapse every issue
into a single pass/fail score. [checks.json](checks.json) records the arithmetic.

## What this establishes and what remains

Stanyan provides a concrete precedent for comparing designs within a funding
budget, tracking finance applications and fallback capital, reviewing costs
against comparable projects, evaluating annual operations and services, and
carrying named conditions to the next decision. It also demonstrates that an
official memo can contain unresolved numerical and narrative inconsistencies.

We have not reproduced every Excel formula, reconciled the complete final legal
and funding stack, independently certified zoning or physical fit, verified final
costs or measured occupancy, service delivery and resident outcomes. The 2025
document remains prospective about lease-up. This case study cannot convert
those projections into observations.

The most useful next implementation is a small **versioned budget and assumption
comparison** which preserves domains, funding status, restrictions, exceptions
and source conflicts. For Jerome, it must use Open Door/co-op inputs and buyer
access assumptions. The specific bridge is set out in [the Bronx comparison](bronx-comparison.md).
