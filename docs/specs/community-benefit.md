# Proposal: practitioner-led housing and community-benefit comparison

Status: broader proposal, 29 October 2026. A first bounded
[conditional housing-cost comparison](../../housing_benefit/README.md) is now
implemented with illustrative programs. It does not establish eligibility, delivery
or causal community benefit. The broader scope below remains proposed and
professional validation remains pending. The subsequent
[RER preparation module](../../housing_rer/README.md) maps pinned instructions into
incomplete five-section drafts, keeping non-equivalent data and missing material
explicit. It does not establish RER compliance. This proposal follows the
[project tenets](../tenets.md) and [research](../../research/community-benefit/report.md).

## Purpose and public description

The Open Housing Toolkit helps housing practitioners and their agents assemble
public data, apply established methods and prepare evidence-backed scenario
comparisons and proposal materials. It brings existing housing, planning and
community resources into inspectable workflows, with sources, assumptions and
professional judgments kept visible. Practitioners can adapt the pieces to their
own questions and methods.

The project is being developed while we learn the professional process. We seek
expert feedback on whether we have represented methods correctly and whether the
outputs help real work. Our contribution is the tooling that makes available
resources easier to use together. We do not claim a complete housing-agency process,
a superior substitute for domain expertise, or a validated way to maximize community
benefit.

For this module, the task is:

> Given a known site and two explicit housing programs, help a practitioner explain
> what each offers, whom it could serve, what may be lost, what is uncertain, and
> what needs further investigation.

The result supports proposal development and discussion. It is not an official
agency submission, entitlement determination or automatically selected best project.

## Foundation: use established resources for their proper role

The selection below is grounded in the research registry. It is our proposed
foundation, not an expert-endorsed finding that these are the only or universally
best resources. Record alternatives and revise selection with practitioners.

| Resource | What we reuse | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NYC Racial Equity Report guide][rer] | Reporting structure connecting project affordability, community context and fair-housing interpretation | Produce an RER-inspired comparison. Applicability and formal compliance require separate assessment. |
| [HPD's EDDE overview][edde] | Existing demographic, housing and equity context | Underlying exports/API are not integrated. Neighborhood vulnerability is not a project-caused displacement forecast. |
| [CEQR socioeconomic guidance][ceqr5] | A future-without-project comparison and direct/indirect impact distinctions | Regulatory significance screens are not benefit scores or findings of no harm. |
| [Urban Institute TOD indicators][tod] | Housing, access and neighborhood outcome dimensions; planning/delivery/evaluation distinctions | Indicators need local definitions and priorities; no universal weighting scheme. |
| [HPD income/rent resources][ami] and applicable program terms | Versioned reference schedules and program-specific definitions | Table vintages, household/bedroom sizes and utility treatment must align. Do not infer affordability from a label alone. |
| [ANHD community principles toolkit][anhd] | A process for communities to articulate needs and criteria | Community preferences and consensus cannot be inferred from APIs or generated by an agent. |
| [MTA developer resources][mta] and established routing tools | A later route to reproducible opportunity-access context | Routing, service coverage and accessibility assumptions need explicit methods; proximity alone is insufficient. |

For every adopted method, record its author/publisher, source/version, relevant
section, applicable question, required data, implementation mapping, known limits,
adaptations and professional review status. Official guidance, empirical evidence,
stakeholder priorities and software documentation play different roles.

A new calculation or adaptation must say which part is established guidance and
which part is our implementation choice. Referencing a source is not proof that we
have interpreted or implemented it correctly.

## First user, decision and scope

The first user is a housing practitioner or knowledgeable member of the housing
community: for example, an agency analyst, planner, developer or community housing
organization. They bring a site, a question and the context for judging the result.

The hero workflow remains **known lot → evidence → scenario/model → proposal
packet → optional assets**. Candidate-site discovery is an upstream extension.
Community-benefit comparison becomes a replaceable analytical module within that
workflow. The practitioner can supply an existing program instead of regenerating it.

The first thin slice compares affordability offers and existing-community questions.
Transit opportunity context is included when an adequately sourced result is supplied;
otherwise it stays unknown. Building a routing engine is not a dependency of the
first affordability comparison. A later adapter can use an established engine.

## Current foundation and missing inputs

Existing components supply verified source packages, typed claim/dependency records,
editable area recipes, explicit assumed massing and information/visual packets.
They do not supply a validated unit mix, affordability program, existing occupancy
inventory, future no-action program or community-benefit estimator.

In particular, area-budget home-equivalents cannot become integer homes by rounding.
The geometry module's gross-floor proxy is not residential zoning floor area.
Existing illustrations do not establish a buildable program or who will benefit.
The new module must request those inputs or preserve their absence.

## P0: a bounded, useful comparison

### Inputs

| Input | Required content and evidence treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Site and question | Parcel identity; decision; population, geography and time horizon; practitioner objective and author |
| Two housing programs | Unit counts by bedroom/tenure; rents and utility treatment; income/program references; restriction duration; source or rationale for each quantity |
| Comparison basis | Existing conditions, a time-specific future no-action baseline, and proposed alternatives kept distinct; author and assumptions |
| Existing-community evidence | Known homes, occupants, businesses/services and protections; coverage limits, possible removals, relocation/return commitments and unresolved questions |
| Reference schedules | Table-level publisher, year/effective date, household/bedroom conventions, units and applicable program rules |
| Optional access result | Method/version, location/network/date, opportunity definition, travel windows, assumptions and limitations; imported as context, not automatically certified |
| Commitments and review | Proposed, binding/secured, financed, delivered or observed status, with supporting instrument where available; practitioner interpretations and disagreements |

A baseline can remain unknown. The module may still show a conditional comparison
between two supplied programs, but cannot label the difference a benefit caused by
the intervention. Missing occupancy does not prohibit all arithmetic; it prevents a
claim that displacement has been ruled out.

Program values may be explicit assumptions while exploring. Their status must carry
through every table and prose claim. A user's professional title alone does not
convert an assumption into an observed fact.

### Processing

1. Verify site identity, program totals, semantic units, schedules and dependency
   bindings. Resolve missing or conflicting reference vintages before dependent
   calculations. Keep project dates distinct from schedule and retrieval dates.
2. Normalize the supplied offer by income band, bedroom size, tenure and duration.
   Compare net additions, removals and preservation only where the baseline and
   categories support those operations.
3. Compute illustrative housing-cost burdens for explicitly defined household
   profiles where rent, income and utility inputs are adequate. State the definition
   and reference threshold; this is not a complete housing-eligibility determination.
4. Place relevant local need and access context alongside the offer. Do not infer
   actual beneficiaries from neighborhood averages, multiply homes by reachable jobs
   to report new jobs, or claim improved transit service from adding housing alone.
5. Assemble direct-loss questions and commitment/delivery status. Keep potential
   gains, harms and unknown indirect effects separate by affected group.
6. Render the same records into the structured comparison and a plain-language packet.
   Practitioner-authored interpretation remains attributed and marked by review scope.

### Outputs

- A typed comparison IR, method/source register and reproducible calculation schedule.
- An affordability-offer table by specified household/program categories, with depth,
  duration and conditional delivery status visible.
- Existing-community/direct-loss coverage and unresolved questions.
- An access-context card if supplied, with gaps rather than invented estimates.
- A benefits/tradeoffs table for current residents, prospective residents,
  businesses/service users and the wider city where evidence supports it.
- A concise narrative for practitioner review, generated from the same records.
- Source, method, assumption and output bindings that identify what becomes stale
  when an input changes.

These describe the broader proposed outputs. The implemented subset and its commands
are documented in the module guide linked above.

## Contracts, modularity and provenance

Use versioned JSON contracts and the existing claim kinds: fact, assumption,
calculation, judgment and unknown. Add interpretation metadata rather than collapsing
context, projections and causal effects into one confidence label.

Each material metric should identify:

`definition + unit + claim kind + evidence interpretation + population/denominator
+ geography + horizon + scenario/baseline + input references + method/version
+ assumptions + uncertainty/missingness + commitment status + review scope`

The module should accept explicitly mapped practitioner inputs or canonical upstream
records, produce its own IR, and publish independently of geometry and rendering.
A universal importer for arbitrary spreadsheets/documents remains later work.

Changing a schedule year, input rent, unit mix, restriction duration or method must
flag dependent comparisons and generated text. Rebuild into a new run. A source
update may require professional reinterpretation rather than automatic acceptance.
Diagnostic packets remain useful while unsupported conclusions remain unavailable.

Do not insert a financial, causal or regulatory method simply because it fits an
arithmetic signature. Its applicability, evidence and validation need their own record.

## Acceptance experiments

The implemented housing-cost subset now exercises relevant checks below in its test
suite; broader contextual inputs, binding commitments and professional acceptance
remain pending. Consult the module guide for the exact supported scope.

| Experiment | Expected behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Larger program, fewer deeply affordable homes | Expose the tradeoff; do not select the larger project automatically. |
| Income schedule and rent schedule have different vintages | Identify the conflict; do not silently blend them. An explicit compatibility determination must be documented. |
| Unknown present occupancy | Preserve unknown direct-loss coverage; prohibit a no-displacement conclusion. |
| No established future no-action baseline | Permit labeled exploratory comparison; no claim of project-attributable improvement. |
| Changed utility assumption or affordability duration | Recompute dependent comparisons and flag narrative/packet versions as stale. |
| Only an area-budget quotient is supplied | Request explicit unit mix; do not manufacture program counts. |
| Transit proximity supplied without routed access | Keep it a proximity observation; do not produce a job-access or travel-savings estimate. |
| Program restriction is proposed but not secured | Preserve proposed commitment status in every output. |
| Conflicting practitioner interpretations | Retain attribution, evidence and unresolved disagreement. |
| Same retained inputs replayed | Deterministic calculations and exports; no claim that this establishes semantic correctness. |

Technical review checks contracts, arithmetic, dependencies and exports. Professional
review checks appropriate methods, missing factors, definitions and interpretation.
A first-time user's ability to understand and correct the packet is a separate test
of usefulness. Record all three outcomes independently.

Expert feedback should identify concrete discrepancies and be incorporated as
versioned method/contract changes. Formal submission, professional acceptance and
usefulness remain pending until actually assessed. Reference citations do not imply
that an agency, research author or advisor has reviewed this implementation.

## Later slices, guided by practitioners

- A pinned-network transit-access adapter using an established routing engine and
  complete relevant feeds, with service/calendar/accessibility limits exposed.
- Household housing-plus-transport budget sensitivities for defined profiles and
  alternative locations.
- Public-resource accounting under an applicable established method, with financial
  flows distinguished from societal welfare.
- Selected comparisons against professional work, and observation of first-time use.
- Any causal rent/displacement/emissions model only after defining treatment,
  population, comparison, horizon, evidence and a credible evaluation design.

Overall rankings require an attributed objective and reviewed treatment of tradeoffs,
uncertainty and missing harms. They are not the default output of this proposal.
New proposal or image assets must inherit the analytical limits; rendering quality
cannot count as evidence of community benefit.

## What we need from contributors

Help us identify the resources practitioners actually use, correct our interpretation,
map real input/output examples into the contracts, and tell us whether the packet
supports a decision. A useful correction may be a missing step, a mistaken unit,
a different baseline or an interpretation that needs professional judgment.

The promise we can make is to keep those corrections legible and reproducible.
Whether this saves meaningful practitioner work is a hypothesis to test together.
